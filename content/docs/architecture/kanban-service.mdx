title: Kanban service
description: The board at port 5000 — its swimlanes, its single state source, its auto-refresh contract, and the decision API.

The Kanban service holds the factory's board state and serves it at **port
5000**. It is the component people look at, and the only one that accepts a
review decision.

## State

Work items are **persisted**, and the board reads the same records the
orchestrator writes. There is no board-side copy of the truth to drift out of
sync: what the board shows is the state the factory holds, refreshed.

Each work item carries its project, its source issue, its swimlane, its round
count, and — once a build has finished — the build results a reviewer needs.

## Swimlanes

| Swimlane | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Backlog** | Polled from GitHub, not yet accepted. |
| **Frontier** | Accepted, waiting for a worker. |
| **In Progress** | A worker container is open on it. |
| **Review** | Build finished, awaiting a decision. |
| **Done** | Approved and merged. |

**Rejected** and **escalated** are terminal states off the main flow. Both are
rendered on the board; the factory does not hide failures from the surface
people watch.

## Auto-refresh

The board auto-refreshes and shows real-time status. The design point is not
convenience — it is that a board someone has to refresh manually is a board
they stop trusting. Auto-refresh keeps the board honest against the factory's
actual state, which matters most when a build is failing and someone is
watching it fail.

## Decisions

A ticket in **Review** exposes three actions, and they are the board's whole
write path:

- **Approve** — hands the ticket to the merger, which opens and merges the pull
  request. The ticket moves to Done.
- **Request changes** — attaches the feedback and returns the ticket to the
  build; the round counter increments.
- **Reject** — moves the ticket to the rejected state; the factory stops work
  on it.

Feedback timeout also moves a ticket: past the threshold, the factory merges
rather than holding the pipeline.

## Multi-project on one board

Tickets are grouped or filterable by project, so a reviewer can read one
repository's queue. The board is shared; the view of it does not have to be.
