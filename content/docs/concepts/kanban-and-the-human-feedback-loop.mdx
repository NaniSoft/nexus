---
title: The Kanban and the human feedback loop
description: Five swimlanes, one board, three decisions — approve, request changes, reject — and a hard ceiling of three rounds.
---

The Kanban board is where the factory becomes visible and where a human takes
over. It is a board at port 5000 with five swimlanes and auto-refresh, and it
is the only place work is approved.

## The five swimlanes

| Swimlane | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Backlog** | Issues polled from the configured repositories, not yet started. |
| **Frontier** | Work items the factory has accepted and is about to build. |
| **In Progress** | Live builds — a worker container is open on the issue. |
| **Review** | Finished builds waiting for a human decision. |
| **Done** | Approved and merged. |

Two further states exist off the main flow: a **rejected** state for tickets a
human declines, and **escalated** for tickets that failed hard enough to leave
the normal loop. Both are visible on the board — nothing disappears into a log.

## The three decisions

A ticket in Review presents its diff and its build result, and offers exactly
three actions.

**Approve** — the factory opens the pull request and merges it. Approval is the
only path to Done.

**Request changes** — the ticket returns to the build with the feedback
attached, and the round counter increments. Three rounds is the ceiling.

**Reject** — the ticket moves to the rejected state and the factory stops
spending on it.

## Why three rounds

The ceiling exists to prevent the failure mode every unbounded feedback loop
has: the agent and the reviewer disagreeing forever while the factory keeps
paying for containers. Three rounds is enough for a reviewer to steer a build
that is close and cheap to abandon for one that is not. When the third round
comes back and feedback is still outstanding, the loop closes — see
[the auto-merge policy](/docs/concepts/kanban-and-the-human-feedback-loop#the-auto-merge-policy).

## The auto-merge policy

Auto-merge is the factory's answer to a bottleneck it cannot schedule around: a
reviewer who is not looking. The design merges on approval, and merges on
feedback timeout — if a ticket sits in Review past the threshold, the factory
merges rather than holding the pipeline.

That is an aggressive default and it is stated plainly so it can be governed.
The board is where the threshold is visible, the board is where the decision
was supposed to happen, and the merge is always a pull request that can be
reverted. A factory that silently stops is worse than one that ships a
revertible change.

## The board as a control surface

Auto-refresh means the board reflects reality without anyone polling it, and it
carries the factory's health as well as its work: escalated tickets, failed
builds, and round counts all appear where the work is. That is deliberate —
[observability](/docs/architecture/observability-and-error-handling) at the
factory level is most useful at the moment someone is looking at a ticket.
