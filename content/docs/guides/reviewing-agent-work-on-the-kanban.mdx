---
title: Reviewing agent work on the Kanban
description: The three decisions, what to read before making one, and how the three-round ceiling changes the conversation.
---

Reviewing is the factory's human job. A build arrives in **Review** with its
diff and its build results, and you make one of three decisions. Everything
else about the role follows from taking that seriously.

## What is in front of you

A Review ticket carries what happened, not a summary of it: the files that
changed, the commands that ran, and the outcomes of the project's own tests —
all produced inside [the container that built
it](/docs/architecture/worker-container-lifecycle).

Read the tests first. The factory runs the project's suite, so a green run is
evidence, but it is evidence about what the project already tests. A change
with no test touching it is unverified no matter how green the run is.

Then read the diff as a whole. Agent work fails in characteristic ways — a
change that solves the issue in one place while quietly breaking an assumption
somewhere else, or a fix that adds a special case where the codebase already has
an abstraction.
Whole-diff reading is what catches those.

## The three decisions

**Approve** merges. Do it when the diff answers the issue and you would have
merged it from a colleague. Approval is a signature on the change, not a
thank-you to the factory.

**Request changes** sends the ticket back with your feedback attached and
consumes one of the three rounds. Write the feedback as you would for a
colleague's pull request: specific, actionable, and pointed at the change
rather than the agent. "This is wrong" costs a round and produces another
guess; "this path drops the error case — the caller in X assumes it is set"
produces a fix.

**Reject** ends the ticket. Use it when the issue itself turns out to be wrong,
duplicated, or not worth the remaining rounds. Rejection is not a judgement on
the build; it is the decision that the work should not continue.

## Three rounds, so spend them well

The [round ceiling](/docs/concepts/kanban-and-the-human-feedback-loop#why-three-rounds)
exists so that disagreement terminates. It also changes what good feedback
looks like: three rounds is enough to steer a build that is close, and not
enough to design something by committee through the board.

If you are asking for changes a second time, stop and consider whether the
issue was buildable. If it was not, the honest move is
[reject and rewrite](/docs/guides/writing-an-issue-the-factory-can-build#the-ceiling-is-a-feature)
rather than a third round aimed at a target that keeps moving.

## The auto-merge threshold

Tickets left in **Review** past the feedback threshold merge without you. That
is the factory's answer to a review queue nobody is reading, and it is worth
understanding before you rely on it: Review is a place where work is waiting
for a decision, not a place where it is safely parked. A ticket you are not
ready to decide is a ticket to reject or to approve.
